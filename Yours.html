Yours, yesterday

A letter unfolded from the past

The exhibits tell stories of how the Philippines was shaped by trade, cultural exchanges, traditions, and the struggles of our ancestors. From the agricultural products that traveled across oceans to the weapons used to protect communities, each artifact represents a different part of our history. The old books show how language and beliefs developed
through cultural encounters, while thegalleon represents the connections
formed between distant countries.These exhibits also remind us that the
past continues to influence our present.The food we eat, the words we use, and
some of the traditions we practicecarry traces of these historical
exchanges. Through these displays,we realized that our present way of
life is deeply connected to theexperiences and decisions of the
generations before us.
The galleon immediately caught our attention the moment we entered the museum. It was not only because of its enormous size, but also because visitors were given the opportunity to enter and explore it. Seeing it up close made us appreciate the amount of effort, planning, and craftsmanship that must have gone into building such a massive vessel. Knowing that ships like these once traveled across vast oceans made the experience even more fascinating. However, aside from the galleon, we were also particularly drawn to the spice exhibit. Different spices were placed in separate jars, allowing visitors to smell and recognize their distinct scents. Perhaps this caught our interest because we enjoy interactive exhibits that allow us to experience something beyond simply looking at an object. It made history feel more engaging and personal. We realized that being able to explore, touch, and smell certain elements of the past creates a different kind of appreciation and understanding. It was no longer just about learning what happened centuries ago, but about experiencing a small part of what people might have encountered during that time.
Through this experience, we learned that Filipino identity is shaped by a combination of our own traditions and the different cultural influences we encountered throughout history. Our language, food, beliefs, and practices reflect generations of interactions with other nations, particularly during the period of the Manila-Acapulco Galleon Trade. However, we also realized that our identity is not defined solely by the influences of foreign cultures. The exhibits, particularly the traditional Filipino weapons and early writings, reminded me that we already had our own traditions, skills, and ways of life long before these encounters. This experience made us appreciate that being Filipino means carrying a history of resilience, adaptation, and cultural diversity while recognizing the roots that make our identity distinct.

As members of the younger generation, we can contribute to heritage preservation by taking an active interest in our history and sharing what we learn with others. We can start by visiting museums, historical landmarks, and cultural sites in our communities, while also encouraging our friends and families to appreciate these places. We can use social media and other digital platforms to share information about Filipino traditions, historical events, and local cultural practices, making them more accessible to other young people.
